# Closed-Loop Detection Engineering for Vigil

## A customer-owned red/blue learning loop grounded in LogLM

Author: Evan Powell, DeepTempo Date: September 4, 2026 Status: Design proposal for the Vigil-SOC/vigil roadmap

---

## Summary

NVIDIA and CrowdStrike published a validation-first offensive-defensive agentic system: red agents adapt attacks inside a representative environment, blue agents reconstruct the telemetry, author candidate detections, and pass each candidate through a gauntlet of deterministic checks before it reaches the detection engine. The loop repeats, forcing offense onto a harder path each cycle. Their result rested on two things that are portable and one that is not. Portable: the harness discipline (schema grounding, telemetry grounding, artifact linting, replay, independent review) and the closed loop itself. Not portable, and not what we want: the reliance on a specific fine-tuned Nemotron pair. Their gains came from the complete pipeline, not from an isolated model swap, which is the whole point for us. The harness is the moat, not the weights.

Vigil is already most of the way to the blue half of that loop and owns the substrate the loop needs. It has a hypothesis-driven hunt with an append-only Ledger and a critic that argues the null, a detections domain over 7,200 rules with coverage and gap analysis, an approval domain with confidence-gated autonomy, a workflow engine that composes agents from plain-text Playbooks, and LogLM wired in as an AI-native detection layer through MCP and pgvector. What is missing is: the red half as an optional capability, a validation harness that gates authored detections the way NVIDIA's six mechanisms do, and a durable record that ties an attack to what saw it, what missed it, and what was authored in response so the system tightens over time rather than forgetting each cycle.

This document proposes how to build that, model-agnostic throughout (bring your own LLM), customer-owned end to end (no telemetry egress, the loop runs where the data is), and grounded in LogLM wherever LogLM earns its place. It then proposes three phases of GitHub Issues, matched to how the Vigil repo actually authors work.

---

## What the NVIDIA/CrowdStrike loop does, reduced to its mechanisms

Stripped of the model specifics, their loop is four stages and six harness mechanisms.

Four stages: execute and capture (red runs an attack path in a representative environment, action trace plus sensor telemetry recorded); process and reconstruct (blue receives the trace and telemetry, determines what could be reconstructed, which existing detections fired, where the gaps are); generate and validate (blue authors candidate detections, a validation harness checks and backtests each, returns failures for repair, ships what passes); retest and adapt (an independently seeded attack retests the objective, detection context returns to red, which explores evasions until no viable path remains).

Six harness mechanisms that make authored detections trustworthy rather than merely plausible: a schema knowledge base so agents cannot invent fields; telemetry grounding so detections rest on observed events; specialized detection authoring separated from orchestration; artifact linting that rejects syntax errors and, critically, detections tied to specific IPs, hosts, users, or subnets, forcing behavioral signals instead; detection replay against captured telemetry so a candidate that matches nothing is rejected; and independent review by a judge with fresh context for behavioral alignment and robustness.

The two evaluation gates matter as much as the mechanisms: backtest (does it catch the attack that created it) then live-fire (does it generalize to unseen executions of the same behavior, and does it stay quiet on benign traffic). A detection that passes backtest but fails live-fire is the trap the whole harness exists to catch.

Our job is to reproduce those mechanisms as Vigil-native capabilities, keep them model-agnostic, and add the one thing their post appropriately treated as out of scope for a general platform: a detection layer that also catches what no rule will ever be written for, which is where LogLM enters.

## Where Vigil already stands

The loop is not a greenfield build. The relevant substrate exists.

The append-only execution log NVIDIA's loop needs, Vigil already has as the **Ledger**: `agent_events`, a Postgres table with a `(run_id, seq)` primary key, seq assigned server-side inside an advisory-locked transaction so no caller chooses its own position, `payload` and `snapshot` as JSONB, and a closed `run_kind` set. The **Projection** is folded from the Ledger on read and never stored, so there is no second copy of a run's state to drift. This is exactly the substrate a learning loop should be built on: an immutable record of what each run did, cheap to append, replayable, owned by the customer's own Postgres. Issue \#727 (the episodic-memory epic) shows the maintainers already intend to mine this log for what runs saw and concluded. The closed loop is the natural consumer of that same record.

The blue half is largely present. The **hunt** run kind is hypothesis-driven with a lead that emits one decision per turn from a closed vocabulary, workers that gather evidence, and a **critic** that argues the strongest benign explanation before any hypothesis reaches proven. That critic is, in spirit, NVIDIA's independent-review mechanism already in the tree, applied to hunt verdicts rather than to authored detections. The **detections** domain manages rule sources (Sigma, Splunk ESCU, Elastic, KQL today) and exposes `analyze_coverage`, `identify_gaps`, `search_detections`, and coverage stats. The **MITRE Analyst** agent already does technique identification, coverage analysis, gap prioritization, and detection-template generation. The **approval** domain gates action on confidence, embodying the house rule that the system can only demote itself and only a human promotes autonomy.

LogLM is wired in, not bolted on. Findings carry LogLM provenance; the ingestion path has a dedicated `loglm` schema kind that turns embedding-export Parquet rows into Findings with source evidence; pgvector backs embeddings and `nearest_neighbors`; and there is an authenticated LogLM MCP surface with its own `loglm.view` grant. LogLM is therefore reachable both as a detection signal (it produces Findings) and as a queryable neighborhood function (embedding similarity), which are the two ways the loop will use it.

What is genuinely missing is three things: a **red** execution capability and harness (Vigil has no offensive capability today; VStrike is topology enrichment, not attack execution); a **detection validation harness** (nothing lints for environment-specific strings, replays a candidate against captured telemetry, or runs an independent detection judge); and the **loop controller and its durable record** that ties a red run to the blue response and drives the next iteration.

## Design principles, non-negotiable

**Model-agnostic.** Every reasoning step runs through the existing `LLMRouter` and Bifrost surface. The loop must run end to end on a local Ollama model, a frontier API model, or anything in between. We reproduce NVIDIA's harness discipline, not their fine-tuned weights; the specialized-authoring role is a prompt-and-grant configuration of whatever model the operator chose, not a required custom model. This is a feature, not a compromise: their own result showed the harness carried the gain.

**Customer-owned, no egress.** The representative environment, the attack traces, the telemetry, the authored detections, and the execution log all live in the customer's Postgres and object store. The loop is the sharpest possible expression of "send the model to the data": red, blue, LogLM, and the Ledger are all inside the boundary. Nothing about a customer's environment, detections, or the paths their defenses miss leaves that boundary. This is a structural advantage a SaaS red/blue service cannot match, because that service would have to pull the customer's telemetry and detection posture into a vendor cloud to run the loop at all.

**Append-only, replayable, auditable.** The loop writes to the Ledger and nowhere authoritative else. Every attack executed, every detection that fired or missed, every candidate authored, every validation verdict, every promotion decision is an event with a seq. The "airtight over time" property the request asks for is exactly this: coverage is a fold over an immutable log, so it can only be understood, replayed, and improved, never silently lost. A promotion is a human-authored event, honoring demote-yourself-only.

**LogLM where it earns it, not everywhere.** LogLM is not a universal hammer. It has three specific, defensible jobs in this loop, named precisely below. Rule-based detection remains the breadth layer for known technique patterns; LogLM is the depth layer for the novel and the unseen. The honest architecture is both, each doing what it is good at.

**Behavioral, never brittle.** We adopt NVIDIA's hardest linting rule directly: a candidate detection tied to a specific IP, host, user, or subnet is rejected and returned for rewrite around behavior. This is what makes a detection generalize from backtest to live-fire, and it is the difference between a coverage number that means something and one that memorized the test.

**Optional and pluggable, off by default.** The entire red/blue auto-detection-engineering capability, ARTEMIS included, is an add-on, not a core feature. A Vigil install that never enables it behaves exactly as it does today: no red run to authorize, no offensive engine to trust, no new surface to secure. This is not only prudence about an offensive capability that many operators will not want switched on; it is the honest way to ship something this new. The rest of this document is written to that constraint, and the section below says how the capability plugs in rather than bolts on.

## The loop, as Vigil concepts

Five areas of effort. They map onto the request's four bullets and add the controller and record that make it a loop rather than a one-shot.

### 1\. Context and red-team planning

**Goal.** Produce an attack plan grounded in two contexts: the environment (assets, segments, mission systems, the topology VStrike already fuses onto Findings) and the existing detection posture (what Vigil's rules cover, where `identify_gaps` says coverage is thin, and what LogLM has historically flagged in this environment). The plan is threat-informed and objective-driven, in MITRE ATT\&CK terms, deliberately biased toward the seams the current posture leaves open.

**Why context-aware matters.** An uninformed red plan wastes iterations on techniques already well covered. A plan that reads coverage and gap analysis first spends its budget where the defense is actually weak, which is what makes each loop tighten the specific environment rather than rehearse generic attacks.

**Vigil shape.** A red **planning role**, a lead that plans in the register of the hunt lead's arch, running as a phase in the loop's Compose Playbook (or, if promoted later, as its own `red` run kind). Planning inputs: the MITRE Analyst's coverage output, `detections.identify_gaps`, VStrike topology from `entity_context`, and a LogLM-derived summary of what has been anomalous here before. Execution is delegated to an offensive engine through a new **Vendor Slice** with an MCP server, exactly the integration pattern every other tool follows, so it is optional and unconfigured-means-absent by construction. The slice defines an engine-neutral `OffensiveEngine` contract (plan in, action trace and captured telemetry out), and **ARTEMIS ships as the batteries-included default adapter behind it, batteries included but not required**: the contract stays open so Caldera or a bring-your-own harness can be substituted, and nothing in the loop imports ARTEMIS directly. ARTEMIS (Stanford-Trinity, `Automated Red Teaming Engine with Multi-agent Intelligent Supervision`) fits the contract cleanly: it is driven by a `python -m supervisor.supervisor --config-file <plan>.yaml` entrypoint, so the adapter compiles the red lead's plan to that YAML; it runs a supervisor over spawned Codex-based sub-agents (`spawn_codex`) with a triager, deployable locally or in Docker; and it writes persistent logs per run, which are the action trace reconstruction consumes. Its model config is OpenAI/OpenRouter-compatible, so the adapter points ARTEMIS's supervisor and sub-agents at Vigil's own Bifrost surface. That keeps even the offensive engine on the operator's chosen model and inside the boundary, the same model-agnostic, no-egress posture the rest of the loop holds. The plan and every executed step are Ledger events.

**Autonomy tier.** Offensive execution is `stop`/`pause` by default: a red run against a real or representative environment requires explicit human authorization and runs against the designated environment only. This is the approval domain doing what it already does, applied to the sharper edge.

**LogLM here:** grounding the plan. LogLM's history of what has been anomalous in this environment tells the planner where behavior is already legible to the depth layer and where it is dark, so the plan can probe the dark.

**The LLM plane: how the Codex sub-agents reach Bifrost.** Bifrost is the reason the ARTEMIS integration is a pointer rather than a translation layer, because ARTEMIS already speaks what Bifrost serves. Bifrost is Vigil's LLM gateway, exposing an OpenAI-format surface at `${BIFROST_URL}/v1`; the agent-layer harness and `core/llm/router/router.py` both build their clients against that same base URL, and Bifrost fans each request out to whatever provider the operator configured, holding the real provider keys itself. ARTEMIS's Codex-forked sub-agents are, by construction, OpenAI-compatible clients: a base URL, a key, a model name. So the adapter sets the sub-agents' base URL to Bifrost's `/v1` and hands them one Bifrost key. When the supervisor calls `spawn_codex` and a sub-agent issues a chat-completions call, that call lands on Bifrost, not on OpenAI: Bifrost resolves the model name to the configured backend, applies its own key, meters the spend, and returns. Every reasoning token the offensive engine burns, supervisor and all concurrent sub-agents alike, traverses the same gateway the blue side uses.

Four properties follow, and they are the point. Model-agnostic on the red side: point Bifrost's resolution at a local model and the whole pentest runs on it, point it at a frontier API and it runs there, and ARTEMIS never knows the difference, so bring-your-own-LLM holds for offense without forking ARTEMIS. No egress, conditionally: if the resolved provider is in-boundary, no reasoning traffic leaves the environment. This last clause is worth stating exactly, because ARTEMIS's own default posture is OpenAI/OpenRouter, which would exfiltrate every prompt; routing through Bifrost is what closes that, and it should be enforced, not assumed. Key hygiene: ARTEMIS carries a single Bifrost key, provider secrets stay in Vigil's store, and a red-engine container is never a place a real Anthropic or OpenAI key sits. One meter: offensive spend reconciles through the same pricing and telemetry path as blue runs, which is what lets a red run's action trace and its token cost both settle against one `run_id` in the Ledger.

Two planes stay separate. Bifrost carries only the LLM plane. ARTEMIS's actual offensive actions, the tools it runs and the packets it sends, go to the designated target environment and never touch Bifrost. The reasoning is in-boundary through the gateway; the attack traffic is against the range. Conflating the two is the easy mistake.

Three edges to design against, all real. Endpoint shape: confirm Bifrost's `/v1` covers the exact surface the Codex fork uses (chat-completions versus the Responses API, and tool-call formatting); if the fork speaks Responses, either pin the sub-agents to chat-completions in the adapter or ensure Bifrost proxies that shape. Rate isolation: ARTEMIS runs as an external process, so it bypasses the agent layer's in-process Limiter and leans entirely on Bifrost for limits; without its own bucket, a peak of eight concurrent sub-agents can starve the blue-side hunt and investigate runs sharing the gateway. The fix is a dedicated Bifrost virtual key with its own rate bucket for the red engine, which also makes offensive spend separable in whatever usage view we build later. Double retry: the agent layer sets `maxRetries: 0` and makes its limiter the retry policy, while ARTEMIS has its own retry logic, so against Bifrost both plus the gateway can compound and bill every attempt; ARTEMIS's retry should be set conservatively.

### 2\. Observe the attack through the detection layer(s)

**Goal.** For each executed attack step, determine what the defense saw. Two layers, run together: the existing detections (the Suricata, Sigma, Splunk, Elastic, KQL corpus, customized per customer) and, where deployed, LogLM. The output is a per-step verdict: detected by a named rule, detected by LogLM, detected by both, or missed. "Missed" is the signal the rest of the loop consumes.

**Why both layers.** This is the core of the honest detection story. Rules catch known technique patterns with precision and explainability. LogLM catches the compound and the novel: the attack step for which no rule was ever written, seen as anomalous behavior in high-dimensional embedding space. An attack step missed by every rule but flagged by LogLM is not a coverage gap to panic over; it is LogLM doing its job, and it is also the ideal seed for authoring a durable rule (see area 4). An attack step missed by both is the true gap and the highest-priority authoring target.

**Vigil shape.** This is a **reconstruction** phase in the blue half, analogous to NVIDIA's "process and reconstruct." It reads the red run's action trace and the telemetry the attack produced (already flowing into Vigil as Findings through ingestion, including the `loglm` schema path), correlates attack steps to Findings, and queries the detections domain for which rules matched. It writes a reconstruction event per step to the Ledger, citing evidence the way the hunt's evidence-relations already do. The schema-knowledge and telemetry-grounding mechanisms live here: the agent enumerates available sources and fields before reasoning, so it cannot claim a detection on a field the environment does not emit.

**LogLM here:** as a detection signal. LogLM-origin Findings are first-class in reconstruction; a step they cover is marked covered-by-LogLM, distinct from covered-by-rule, so the loop can reason about which layer is carrying which technique.

### 3\. Decide whether to author new detections

**Goal.** For each gap (missed, or LogLM-only where the operator wants a portable rule as well), offer the operator a choice: author a new detection now, defer it, or accept the gap with a reason. The choice is offered two ways, per the request: up front as a policy for the whole run ("auto-author for any gap above severity X, ask me otherwise"), or in the loop per gap. Every choice is recorded.

**Why a choice, not automatic authoring.** This is the demote-yourself-only rule and the StackStorm lesson made concrete. Authoring and promoting a detection changes what fires in the customer's SOC. The system may propose freely; promotion into the live detection set is a human-granted step, or an explicitly pre-authorized policy the human set. Recording the decision, including "accepted the gap because this is sanctioned scanning," is what makes the coverage posture auditable rather than mysterious.

**Vigil shape.** A checkpoint in the loop, using the existing checkpoint/resolution machinery the hunt already has (the same mechanism that stops for operator approval). The up-front policy is run configuration; the in-loop choice is a checkpoint event answered by a resolution event. Confidence-gated auto-authoring reuses the approval domain's thresholds (the README's auto-approve above 0.90, review below 0.85 pattern).

**LogLM here:** informing the choice. LogLM's confidence and the neighborhood of similar past Findings help the operator judge whether a gap is worth a rule or already owned by the depth layer.

### 4\. Author, ground, and validate the detection

**Goal.** Author a candidate detection for the chosen gap, ground it so it is testable and behavioral, and gate it through a validation harness that reproduces NVIDIA's six mechanisms before anything reaches the live detection set.

**The validation harness, mechanism by mechanism, as Vigil components.** Schema knowledge: the detections domain already knows the rule formats; the authoring agent is given the target format's schema and the environment's available fields as grounding, so it cannot emit an invalid or unsupported rule. Specialized authoring: a dedicated authoring role (a configuration of the operator's chosen model with a tight prompt and narrow grants), separated from the loop's orchestration context, matching NVIDIA's bounded-expert separation without requiring a bounded-expert model. Artifact linting: automated checks reject syntax errors and, the hard rule, any detection keyed to a specific IP, host, user, or subnet, returning guidance to rewrite around behavior. Replay: the candidate is run against the captured attack telemetry from area 2; if it does not match the activity it was written for, it is rejected and returned for repair. Independent review: a judge role with fresh context, exactly like the hunt's critic, evaluates behavioral alignment, robustness, and multi-signal use. Failures at any stage return structured feedback for another attempt, bounded by a repair budget (NVIDIA capped repair attempts; we should too, through the existing budget mechanism).

**Then the two evaluation gates.** Backtest is the replay above: does it catch the attack that created it. Live-fire: an independently seeded red run of the same objective (area 1 again, new seed) retests, and the candidate must fire on the new execution and stay quiet on the environment's benign traffic. Only a detection that clears both is eligible for promotion. This is what stops us from shipping detections that memorized one attack trace.

**LogLM here, its most important role: grounding new detections.** When LogLM flagged an attack step that no rule caught (the covered-by-LogLM-only case from area 2), LogLM is the ground truth for authoring a portable rule. The embedding neighborhood of the anomalous Finding tells the authoring agent which behavioral features actually separated the attack from normal, which is precisely the behavioral signal the anti-brittleness lint demands. LogLM turns "we know something was wrong here because the model said so" into "here are the behavioral features to write a Sigma rule around." This is the synthesis the request is reaching for: the foundation model does not just detect, it teaches the rule layer what to look for, so coverage that started as model-only intuition becomes an explainable, portable, shareable artifact. It also means a customer with LogLM ends each loop with both a tighter model context and a growing corpus of grounded rules, while a customer without LogLM still gets the rule loop, just without the grounding assist.

**Vigil shape.** The authoring and validation capabilities are importable **Skills** (an authoring skill and a validation skill, categories detection and custom), not a new core slice, so they add as data and are absent when not imported. Each names its `required_tools`: the detection-domain tools for format schemas and, where present, the LogLM MCP tools for grounding. A skill produces candidate detections as artifacts, runs them through the harness, and writes every verdict to the Ledger of the run it is part of. Promotion writes to the detection set through the detections domain and records a promotion event. The one piece that may warrant a small typed home in the detections domain is the candidate-and-validation-record artifact itself, since it is persisted and read across phases; the reasoning that produces it stays in the skills.

### 5\. The loop controller and the durable record

**Goal.** Tie areas 1 through 4 into an iterating loop that halts on a real condition (no further viable attack path within the environment, or budget/operator stop), and persist the whole thing as a foldable record so coverage improves monotonically and auditably.

**Why this is the airtight-over-time property.** A single pass authors some detections. A loop, with a record, does something stronger: each cycle the red planner reads the updated posture (including detections authored last cycle) and is forced onto a harder path, exactly as NVIDIA describes. The record is the mechanism that makes "harder each time" true rather than aspirational, because the planner's context is a fold over every prior cycle's Ledger events. Over many cycles, the set of viable attack paths within the environment shrinks, and the shrinking is visible in the log. That is airtight becoming measurable.

**Vigil shape.** The controller is a **Playbook executed by Compose**, one cycle per run, re-invoked to iterate rather than looping in-engine, with a scheduler triggering the next cycle and the coverage read-model carrying state between them. It reuses the Ledger, the fold, the checkpoint/resolution machinery, and the budget mechanism wholesale, and adds no run kind unless the `red`\-versus-`compose` decision later favors one. The durable record is the Ledger itself plus a thin, optional projection that answers the questions the loop and the operator ask of it: for this environment, what has been attacked, what covers each technique now and by which layer, what remains a gap and why, and how the viable-path frontier has moved cycle over cycle. This projection is the natural sibling of the episodic-memory epic (\#727), and should be built to share its substrate rather than duplicate it.

**LogLM here:** the frontier summary. LogLM's evolving view of what is anomalous in this environment is one honest measure of whether the loop is closing, complementary to the rule-coverage count.

### A note on policy and metrics convergence

This loop introduces several policies, and they should not be scattered across per-run config, per-domain settings, and adapter flags where an operator has to hunt for them. The offensive-execution authorization and environment scope, the author-or-defer thresholds and the "accept the gap" reasons, the confidence gates that govern auto-authoring and promotion, and the red engine's own rate bucket are all governance decisions of the same kind: rules the operator sets that determine how much the system may do on its own before a human is asked. They belong in one place. The same is true of what the loop produces as measurement: spend and token usage by task and by run kind (the single-meter property above is what makes this clean), coverage by technique and by layer, and the viable-path frontier over cycles.

We are not designing that operator surface here, and this document should not grow a dashboard. The point is only to build toward it rather than away from it: define each policy as data with a stable identity rather than as a scattered flag, write each metric against the one `run_id` and the Ledger rather than into a local counter, and keep the red engine's usage separable (the dedicated virtual key does this). Do that and a central policy-and-metrics console becomes an assembly of things that already exist, not a rewrite. Treat this as a constraint on how the pieces below are shaped, not as scope to be built in these phases.

## Making the whole capability optional

The request is to make ARTEMIS and the entire red/blue auto-detection-engineering loop optional, at least for now. This is not a retrofit; it is achievable by choosing to express the capability in Vigil's existing extension points rather than as new core machinery. The short answer to the meta-skill question: do not invent one, borrow what is already there. Vigil already has the three composition primitives a pluggable capability needs, and the loop maps onto them cleanly.

**The three seams already in the tree.** First, **Integrations** are Vendor Slices discovered by scanning `core/integrations/*/descriptor.py` and gated by config and MCP enable flags; an unconfigured integration simply is not present. The offensive engine is therefore optional by construction the moment it is an integration, which is exactly what the ARTEMIS adapter is. Second, **Skills** are importable capability bundles: a skill ships as a `SKILL.md` zip whose frontmatter maps to the skills table (name, category from detection/enrichment/response/reporting/custom, `required_tools`, input and output schemas) and whose body is the prompt, and the skill-tools bridge exposes each active skill to agents as a `skill_<name>` tool at runtime. Import one and agents can use it; deactivate it and they cannot. Third, **Workflows** are authored as **Playbooks** and executed by **Compose**, and a custom Workflow is DB-authored data, not code, with ordered phases each naming an agent and its grants.

**The loop expressed in those seams.** The blue-half capabilities become Skills, not core slices: a reconstruction skill (given a red trace, correlate to Findings and emit per-step detection verdicts), an authoring skill (given a gap, produce a candidate detection), and a validation skill (lint, replay, review, emit a verdict). Each is a bundle an operator imports; each names its `required_tools`, which are the detection-domain tools and, where present, the LogLM MCP tools. The offensive engine is the ARTEMIS integration, gated like every other. The orchestration, the thing that would tempt someone to invent a meta-skill, is a **Playbook executed by Compose**: an authored sequence of phases whose agents are granted the ARTEMIS tool and the three skill-tools. A Playbook that composes skills is precisely what a meta-skill would be, and Compose is precisely the executor for it, so the concept is already named. Borrowing it keeps the boring-and-explicit rule: no new abstraction where an existing one fits.

**Iteration without a new run kind.** Compose runs an authored order and does not loop back, which at first looks like a blocker for a multi-cycle loop. It is not, and the resolution is the pluggable one: a single cycle is one Compose run, and the loop is that run re-invoked, each cycle reading the coverage read-model built from prior cycles' Ledgers so the red plan is forced onto a harder path. Iteration becomes scheduling plus shared memory, not a new engine. This is why the coverage projection was drawn as a sibling of the episodic-memory work: it is the cross-cycle state that lets re-invocation compound. If, later, first-class in-engine iteration proves worth it, it can be added then; it is not needed to ship, and not needed to be optional.

**The minimal core seam, named honestly.** Almost nothing in core has to change for this to exist, which is the whole point. The Ledger already records any run, so a Compose run of the loop is durable for free. Skills, integrations, and custom workflows are all add-as-data. The one genuine question is whether the loop runs under the existing `compose` run kind or earns its own `red` kind. A distinct kind is a small edit to a closed union and buys precise labeling and a natural place to hang offensive-execution safety defaults; running under `compose` with approval-gated offensive tools buys zero core change and slightly less precise labeling. Either is defensible. The recommendation is to start under `compose` with the offensive MCP tools approval-gated (Vigil already approval-gates vendor MCP action tools), and promote to a dedicated `red` kind only if the labeling and safety-default benefits justify touching the union. That keeps the first release as close to pure-plugin as the architecture allows.

**What optional actually delivers.** With this shape, the capability ships as a bundle: the ARTEMIS integration, three importable skills, and one Playbook, plus an optional coverage read-model. An operator who wants it imports the bundle and configures the engine; an operator who does not is unaffected, and there is no dormant offensive surface in their install. The same bundling makes the capability distributable through the community the way other Vigil skills and workflows already are, and makes a lighter default (a Caldera or Atomic Red Team integration behind the same `OffensiveEngine` contract) a drop-in alternative rather than a fork.

## How the pieces sit in the codebase

A one-line placement for each area, respecting the domain boundaries in CONTEXT.md, the vertical-slice rule, and the optional-by-default constraint above. The bias throughout is add-as-data over add-to-core: an integration, three skills, and a Playbook, so the capability is a bundle rather than a core feature.

Red execution: the offensive engine is an engine-neutral Vendor Slice with an MCP server, its batteries-included default adapter living at `core/integrations/artemis/`, gated like every other integration. Reconstruction, authoring, and validation: importable **Skills** (categories detection and custom) whose `required_tools` name the detection-domain tools and, where present, the LogLM MCP tools, exposed to agents through the skill-tools bridge. Orchestration: a **Playbook executed by Compose**, one cycle per run, re-invoked to iterate. Loop record: the existing per-run Ledger plus an optional coverage read-model folded from it, drawn alongside the \#727 episodic-memory work. The only potential core edit is an optional dedicated `red` run kind; the recommended start is under `compose` with offensive MCP tools approval-gated, which touches no core union. Everything reasoning-bearing goes through `LLMRouter`/Bifrost so the loop stays model-agnostic. Everything durable is an `agent_events` append.

## Questions to resolve before implementation

These are the decisions that change the shape of the build, not the ones a contributor can settle inside a PR. Resolve them, or at least record the chosen default and its reversal cost, before Phase 1 opens.

**The representative environment.** What does red run against, and who stands it up? A digital twin, a staging replica, a cyber range, or a bounded window against production under authorization are four different answers with different fidelity and different blast radius. The loop's honesty depends on the environment resembling production; its safety depends on it not being production. This is the single largest decision and it gates the offensive-execution authorization model. Default worth considering: a customer-provided range or staging replica, with production strictly out of scope for the first release.

**Backtest-only versus full live-fire in the first release.** Live-fire (area 4\) requires a second, independently seeded red run per candidate, which multiplies offensive cost and time. Is the first shippable loop backtest-plus-lint-plus-review, with live-fire following once the red seam is proven, or is live-fire non-negotiable from day one? This decides whether Phase 2 can ship value before Phase 3's reseeding exists. It also interacts with cost: a full loop over many gaps is many ARTEMIS runs.

**What "promotion" writes to, and how a bad rule is pulled.** Promotion puts an authored detection into the live set. Does it write to the same detection sources the rule library already manages, into a customer-owned overlay kept separate from upstream Sigma/Splunk/Elastic/KQL, or both? And what is the demotion path when a promoted rule turns noisy in production: automatic on a false-positive threshold, or human-only? Demote-yourself-only argues the promotion is human-gated, but demotion of a bad rule is the reverse direction and may warrant an automatic safety valve. Decide the write target and the rollback before authoring exists, because it determines the detections-domain contract.

**Loop halt conditions and budget.** "No viable path remains" is the ideal terminator, but it is hard to assert with confidence and easy to fake by under-attacking. What are the concrete stopping rules: a per-cycle and whole-loop budget cap, a maximum cycle count, a plateau detector on the frontier metric, operator stop, or a combination? An unbounded loop against a metered gateway is a cost incident waiting to happen. This decides the budget wiring in Phase 3\.

**Environment identity as a first-class key.** Coverage, the frontier, and the durable record are all "per environment." Is an environment a real modeled entity with a stable id that the Ledger, the coverage projection, and VStrike topology all share, or is it implied by run scoping? Getting this wrong makes the cross-cycle record incoherent. It should be decided before the coverage projection is built, and it is the abstraction the central policy-and-metrics console will key on later.

**LogLM-optional degradation.** The design says the rule loop works without LogLM and is stronger with it. What exactly degrades when LogLM is absent: only the grounding assist in area 4 and the frontier summary in area 5, or more? Naming the precise seams keeps the two deployment shapes (with and without LogLM) both first-class rather than leaving the LogLM-less path as an untested afterthought.

**Codex fork licensing and provenance.** The ARTEMIS default pulls in a Codex-derived scaffold and ARTEMIS's own license. Confirm both are compatible with shipping as a default adapter in an Apache-2.0 project, and decide whether ARTEMIS is vendored, pinned as an external dependency the operator installs, or offered as an optional container. This is a legal and packaging decision, not a code one, and it is cheapest to settle first.

**Compose run kind versus a dedicated `red` kind.** The optional-capability path runs the loop as a Compose Playbook over approval-gated offensive tools, touching no core union; a dedicated `red` run kind buys precise labeling and a home for offensive safety defaults at the cost of a core edit. Which ships first is a real decision, not a detail, because it sets how plugin-pure the first release is. Recommended default: start under `compose`, promote to `red` only if labeling and safety defaults justify it.

**Bundle boundary and versioning.** If the capability ships as a bundle (integration plus three skills plus a Playbook plus an optional read-model), what is the unit an operator installs and how is it versioned as a set, given skills version individually and integrations are discovered by descriptor? Decide whether there is one named bundle with a manifest or four independently installed parts, because it shapes the import and upgrade story and how the community redistributes it.

## Key abstractions the design suggests

If the questions above are the decisions, these are the seams worth naming precisely, because each one is where the design stays honest under change. Most already have a home in the tree; the value is in holding them to a single meaning.

**`OffensiveEngine`.** The contract that makes ARTEMIS batteries-included but not required: a plan goes in, an action trace and captured telemetry come out, and no loop code imports a concrete engine. This is the Ousterhout deep-module boundary of the whole red half. If it is right, swapping ARTEMIS for Caldera is a config change; if it leaks ARTEMIS specifics, the swap becomes a rewrite. It is the abstraction most worth getting right first.

**Attack step and its detection verdict.** The atomic unit reconstruction produces: one executed step, correlated to the telemetry it generated, tagged detected-by-rule / detected-by-LogLM / both / missed, with evidence citations. The entire loop reasons over this unit, so its schema is effectively the loop's internal contract between the red, blue, and authoring halves. It is the natural sibling of the existing **Finding** and run-scoped **Evidence** concepts and should be defined in the same register.

**Detection candidate and its validation record.** An authored detection that is not yet promoted, carrying its lint result, replay result, review verdict, and repair history. Keeping the candidate distinct from a live detection, with the validation record attached, is what makes the harness auditable and what lets a rejected candidate teach the next attempt. This is a new typed artifact; it should not be a live rule wearing a status flag.

**Coverage projection and the viable-path frontier.** A fold over the Ledger, per environment, answering what is covered, by which layer, what remains a gap and why, and how the frontier has moved cycle over cycle. It is computed on read like every other Vigil projection, never a stored second copy, and it shares substrate with the episodic-memory epic rather than duplicating it. The frontier is the one metric that expresses "airtight over time" as a number.

**Governance policy as data.** The offensive-execution authorization, the author-or-defer thresholds, the confidence gates, and the rate bucket, each defined as a policy object with a stable identity rather than a scattered flag. This is the abstraction that lets the future central console assemble rather than rewrite, and it is cheap to honor now and expensive to retrofit.

**No new meta-skill; the Playbook is it.** Worth stating as an explicit non-abstraction: the loop's orchestration does not get a new "meta-skill" concept. A Playbook composing skills, executed by Compose, already is a skill made of skills, and inventing a parallel concept would violate the borrow-the-canonical-one rule. The capability plugs in as an integration, a set of importable skills, and a Playbook, which are the composition primitives the tree already has.

**Environment.** A modeled entity with a stable id, shared by the Ledger, the coverage projection, VStrike topology, and the policies above. Whether this earns a full domain or stays a lightweight shared key is one of the open questions, but that it is one named thing rather than several implied scopes is the abstraction to commit to.

## Three phases of GitHub Issues

The Vigil repo authors issues in a recognizable house style, visible on the open and recently closed set (for example \#463, \#467, \#468, \#727). The shape is consistent: a one-line preamble grading the work (Coding, Vigil context, Lane, Good for); a Context paragraph that names the concrete pain and cites files and line ranges; explicit Acceptance criteria as a checklist; a Files list; and Blocked-by relationships. Labels are `area:*`, `type:*`, and `factory:*`, with epics carrying `type:epic`. Milestones group a release. The graders here (module depth, the 500-line rule, vertical slices, explicit dependencies, tests as the grader, runtime types, boring and explicit) are the review bar every one of these issues will be held to, so they are written to satisfy it.

The phasing principle: each phase closes one honest loop that delivers value on its own, so the effort is never a half-built cathedral. Phase 1 makes coverage assessable against real attack telemetry. Phase 2 makes gaps closeable with trustworthy detections. Phase 3 makes the whole thing iterate and remember. Each phase is an epic with scoped child issues; the child issues are sized to one reviewable PR each, per the repo's own slicing discipline.

### Phase 1: See the attack. Reconstruction and the red seam.

**Epic: continuous offense-defense loop, phase 1: attack execution seam (ARTEMIS batteries included) and detection reconstruction.** Labels: `type:epic`, `area:agents`, `area:integrations`, `area:detections`.

Why first: the loop cannot author against gaps it cannot see, and it cannot see gaps without attack telemetry and a reconstruction that says which layer caught what. This phase delivers standalone value even before any authoring exists: a customer can run a sanctioned attack and get a grounded, per-technique report of what their current rules and LogLM actually caught, which is a coverage assessment worth having on its own. It also establishes the red seam as an integration, keeping offensive execution behind an engine-neutral contract and an approval gate, and ships ARTEMIS as the batteries-included default so the capability works out of the box without binding the loop to any one engine, which is the safest possible way to introduce this.

Child issues, each one PR:

- **feat(integrations): engine-neutral offensive-engine Vendor Slice and `OffensiveEngine` contract.** Context names the pattern (every tool is a slice under `core/integrations/<vendor>/` with a descriptor, per CONTEXT.md and ADR-0001) and the boundary-contract rule (nothing in the loop imports a concrete engine; callers go through the protocol). Acceptance: an `OffensiveEngine` protocol (plan in, action trace and captured telemetry out); a descriptor declaring the integration id and MCP server name; the slice passes the integration-registry ratchet test; no caller imports a concrete engine. Autonomy: execution is approval-gated and scoped to a designated environment. Labels: `area:integrations`, `area:architecture`, `type:feature`.  
- **feat(integrations): ARTEMIS default adapter, batteries included but not required.** The batteries-included implementation of the contract above. Context: ARTEMIS is driven by `python -m supervisor.supervisor --config-file <plan>.yaml` and writes persistent per-run logs; the adapter compiles a red plan to that YAML, launches the run (local or Docker), points ARTEMIS's model config at Bifrost so it stays model-agnostic and in-boundary, and returns the persistent logs as the action trace. Acceptance: a scoped ARTEMIS run launches from a compiled plan and returns an action trace the reconstruction phase can read; ARTEMIS's LLM traffic resolves through Bifrost, not an external endpoint, and a startup check fails the run if the sub-agents' base URL is not Bifrost, so no-egress is enforced rather than assumed; the red engine authenticates with its own dedicated Bifrost virtual key on a separate rate bucket, so its concurrent sub-agents cannot starve blue-side runs and its spend is separable; ARTEMIS's own retry is set conservatively so it does not compound against the gateway; the adapter satisfies the `OffensiveEngine` protocol and is selected by config, so an operator can swap it without touching the loop; a contract test runs against a stub engine to prove the loop is not ARTEMIS-bound. Blocked by the contract issue. Labels: `area:integrations`, `type:feature`.  
- **feat(agents): red planning role and arch.** A planning lead modeled on `threathunt.yaml`, runnable as a phase in a Compose Playbook, that plans from coverage and gap context. No new `RUN_KINDS` entry in this issue; the loop runs under `compose` with offensive tools approval-gated, and a dedicated `red` kind is a separate, optional follow-up. Acceptance: the planner appends its plan and steps to `agent_events`; the plan cites `detections.identify_gaps` and VStrike topology; the plan is emitted against the `OffensiveEngine` contract, not against ARTEMIS specifics; behavior tests on the planner's context assembly. Blocked by the contract issue; parallelizable with the ARTEMIS adapter. Labels: `area:agents`, `type:feature`.  
- **feat(detections): reconstruction phase mapping attack steps to detection verdicts.** Reads a red trace, correlates to Findings, queries which rules matched, marks each step detected-by-rule / detected-by-LogLM / both / missed. Acceptance: a reconstruction event per step with evidence citations; schema-and-field grounding so no verdict rests on an unemitted field; a regression test on the missed-detection classification. Labels: `area:detections`, `area:agents`, `type:feature`.  
- **feat(loglm): LogLM as a first-class reconstruction signal.** LogLM-origin Findings are distinguished in reconstruction from rule matches. Acceptance: covered-by-LogLM is a distinct verdict; the reconstruction report separates the two layers. Labels: `area:detections`, `type:feature`.

How it maps to the goal: this is NVIDIA stages 1 and 2 (execute/capture, process/reconstruct) and the request's first two bullets (red plan from context; see attacks via LogLM or existing detections), landed as the smallest thing that is independently useful.

### Phase 2: Close the gap. Authoring, grounding, and the validation harness.

**Epic: continuous offense-defense loop, phase 2: grounded detection authoring with a validation harness.** Labels: `type:epic`, `area:detections`, `area:agents`.

Why second: it depends on Phase 1's per-gap output, and it delivers the payload the whole effort exists for, a trustworthy new detection, without yet requiring the full iterating loop. After Phase 2 a customer can, for a single attack run, be offered gaps and get validated behavioral detections back. This is where LogLM's grounding role lands and where NVIDIA's six mechanisms become code.

Child issues, each one PR:

- **feat(skills): authoring and validation Skills, plus the candidate artifact.** Importable Skills (categories detection and custom) that produce candidate detections and run the harness, rather than a new core slice, so the capability adds as data. The candidate-and-validation-record is a typed artifact with a small home in the detections domain; the reasoning lives in the skills. Acceptance: skills import as `SKILL.md` bundles and appear as `skill_<name>` tools; candidates are typed Pydantic artifacts; authoring goes through `LLMRouter`; deactivating the skills removes the capability with no core residue. Labels: `area:detections`, `area:skills`, `type:feature`.  
- **feat(detections): validation harness: lint, replay, independent review.** The three deterministic gates. The lint rule rejecting IP/host/user/subnet-specific detections is the headline. Replay runs the candidate against Phase 1's captured telemetry. The judge is a fresh-context role modeled on the hunt critic. Acceptance: a candidate keyed to a literal host is rejected with rewrite guidance (regression test); a candidate that matches no captured activity is rejected; each verdict is a Ledger event; repair attempts are budget-bounded. Labels: `area:detections`, `type:feature`.  
- **feat(loglm): LogLM-grounded authoring for model-only gaps.** For a step LogLM flagged and no rule caught, the embedding neighborhood supplies the behavioral features the authoring agent writes the rule around. Acceptance: a model-only gap yields a candidate whose features derive from the LogLM neighborhood, not from environment literals; the candidate clears the behavioral lint. Labels: `area:detections`, `type:feature`.  
- **feat(agents): author-or-defer choice, up front and in the loop.** The checkpoint/resolution flow for the per-gap decision, plus a run-level policy for auto-author thresholds reusing approval confidence gates. Acceptance: an in-loop gap raises a checkpoint answered by a resolution; an up-front policy auto-authors above threshold and records the decision; promotion into the live set is a recorded, human-granted (or pre-authorized) event. Labels: `area:agents`, `area:response`, `type:feature`.

How it maps to the goal: this is NVIDIA stage 3 (generate/validate) and the six mechanisms, plus the request's third and fourth bullets (offer the author choice; use LogLM to ground new detections). The demote-yourself-only rule is enforced at promotion.

### Phase 3: Make it iterate and remember. Loop controller and durable coverage record.

**Epic: continuous offense-defense loop, phase 3: iterating loop and coverage ledger.** Labels: `type:epic`, `area:agents`, `area:database`.

Why last: it composes Phases 1 and 2 into the closed loop and adds the record that makes coverage improve monotonically and visibly. It is deliberately last because an iterating loop over immature stages would just iterate their weaknesses; once each stage is sound, iteration is where the compounding value is.

Child issues, each one PR:

- **feat(workflows): loop Playbook and re-invocation scheduler.** A Compose Playbook whose phases run the red planner and engine, the reconstruction, authoring, and validation skills, and promotion; iteration is the Playbook re-invoked per cycle by a scheduler, reseeding red from the updated posture, not an in-engine loop. Halts on no-viable-path, budget, or operator stop. Acceptance: the loop drives at least two cycles by re-invocation; cycle N+1's plan context includes cycle N's promoted detections read from the coverage read-model; halt conditions are tested. Labels: `area:workflows`, `area:agents`, `type:feature`.  
- **feat(detections): live-fire gate as a loop stage.** The independently seeded retest and the quiet-on-benign check, gating promotion. Acceptance: a detection that passes backtest but fails the reseeded live-fire is not promoted (regression test); benign-traffic noise is checked before promotion. Labels: `area:detections`, `type:feature`.  
- **feat(database): coverage projection over the Ledger.** A fold answering, per environment: what has been attacked, current coverage per technique and by layer, open gaps with reasons, and the viable-path frontier over cycles. Built on the `agent_events` substrate and aligned with the episodic-memory epic (\#727) rather than duplicating it. Acceptance: the projection is computed on read from the Ledger, never a stored second copy; a replay reproduces it; the frontier metric moves across cycles in a seeded test. Blocked by / coordinated with \#727. Labels: `area:database`, `area:agents`, `type:feature`.  
- **feat(loglm): LogLM frontier summary in the coverage record.** LogLM's evolving anomaly view as a complementary closing-signal beside rule coverage. Acceptance: the coverage projection carries a LogLM-derived frontier signal per cycle. Labels: `area:detections`, `type:feature`.

How it maps to the goal: this is NVIDIA stage 4 (retest/adapt/repeat) plus the airtight-over-time property the request asks for, expressed as a fold over an append-only log so improvement is auditable and owned. The coverage projection is the artifact that lets a customer prove their defense is tightening, in their own environment, on their own data.

## What the customer owns at the end

A loop that runs entirely inside their boundary, on whatever LLM they chose, that attacks a representative model of their environment, sees what their rules and LogLM caught, authors behavioral detections grounded in real telemetry and, where LogLM lit the way, in the model's own sense of what was anomalous, validates each one against backtest and live-fire before it fires in production, and records the whole thing as an immutable log they can replay and audit. Each cycle forces the offense onto a harder path and the record shows the viable-path frontier shrinking. ARTEMIS comes batteries included so the red half works on day one, and stays not required so an operator can swap the engine without touching the loop. The moat is the harness and the loop, not any single model or any single offensive engine, which is why bringing your own LLM costs nothing here and why a black-box SaaS red/blue service cannot offer the same thing without pulling your environment into its cloud. That is the Intelligent Defense Platform's thesis, expressed as running code: the model travels to the data, the collective learning accrues in the customer's own append-only record, and the marginal cost of closing the next gap keeps falling.
